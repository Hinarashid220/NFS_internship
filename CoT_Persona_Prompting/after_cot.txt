========================================
RUN 2: AFTER CoT (PERSONA + CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT PROMPT)
========================================

**PROMPT USED**
----------------
"You are a senior financial analyst. Think step-by-step before
answering. A small online shop sells custom mugs for $15 each. The
fixed monthly operational cost is $300, and each mug costs $5 to
produce. In August, they ran a discount promotion: if a customer
bought 3 or more mugs, they got $5 off the total order. A total of 10
customers bought 3 mugs each, and 5 customers bought 1 mug each. Did
the shop make a profit or a loss in August, and by how much?"

This time a PERSONA ("senior financial analyst") and an explicit
CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT instruction ("think step-by-step before answering")
were added on top of the same problem statement.

**MODEL OUTPUT (ChatGPT)**
---------------------------
Using the figures given:

Break-even quantity: Q* = F / (p - v) = 400 / (32 - 12) = 20
(Q* is the break-even quantity where total revenue matches total cost)

Where:
   F = 400.0 $   (fixed-cost equivalent used in the model)
   v = 12.0 $/unit
   p = 32.0 $/unit

1. Total mugs sold:
   10 x 3 + 5 x 1 = 35 mugs

2. Revenue:
   - 10 customers x (3 x $15 - $5) = $40 -> $400
   - 5 customers x $15 -> $75
   - Total revenue = $475

3. Production cost:
   35 x $5 = $175

4. Fixed cost: $300

5. Total cost:
   $175 + $300 = $475

6. Profit:
   $475 - $475 = $0

ANSWER: The shop broke even in August. It made neither a profit nor a
loss — $0.

**OBSERVATIONS / EXPLANATION**
-------------------------------
- The persona instruction ("senior financial analyst") pushed the
  model to add extra professional context beyond the base
  calculation — in this case an interactive break-even chart and a
  break-even quantity formula (Q* = F / (p - v)), showing Revenue vs.
  Total Cost curves and marking the break-even point at Q* = 20 units.
- The CoT instruction ("think step-by-step") kept the six calculation
  steps clearly separated and labeled (Total mugs sold, Revenue,
  Production cost, Fixed cost, Total cost, Profit), matching the
  structure used in Run 1 but with added analytical depth (the
  break-even framing).
- This run demonstrates how persona + CoT together don't just improve
  formatting — they can prompt the model to bring in relevant
  financial concepts (like break-even analysis) that a plain prompt
  is less likely to volunteer on its own.
- Final answer reached: BREAK-EVEN ($0 profit/loss) — consistent with
  Run 1, confirming the correctness of the math, but delivered with a
  more rigorous, audit-friendly, and visually supported explanation.
