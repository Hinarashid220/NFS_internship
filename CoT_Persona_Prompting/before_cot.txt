========================================
RUN 1: BEFORE CoT (STANDARD PROMPT)
========================================

**PROMPT USED**
----------------
"A small online shop sells custom mugs for $15 each. The fixed monthly
operational cost is $300, and each mug costs $5 to produce. In August,
they ran a discount promotion: if a customer bought 3 or more mugs,
they got $5 off the total order. A total of 10 customers bought 3 mugs
each, and 5 customers bought 1 mug each. Did the shop make a profit or
a loss in August, and by how much?"

No persona and no "think step-by-step" instruction was given. This is
the raw problem statement, asked directly.

**MODEL OUTPUT (ChatGPT)**
---------------------------
Let's calculate it step by step.

1. Total mugs sold
   - 10 customers x 3 mugs = 30 mugs
   - 5 customers x 1 mug = 5 mugs
   Total = 35 mugs

2. Revenue
   For the 10 customers buying 3 mugs:
   - Normal price = 3 x $15 = $45
   - Discount = $5
   - Each customer pays = $40
   - 10 x $40 = $400

   For the 5 customers buying 1 mug:
   - 5 x $15 = $75

   Total revenue = $400 + $75 = $475

3. Production cost
   Each mug costs $5 to produce:
   35 x $5 = $175

4. Total costs
   - Production cost = $175
   - Fixed monthly cost = $300
   Total cost = $475

5. Profit/Loss
   Profit = Revenue - Total Cost
   $475 - $475 = $0

ANSWER: The shop neither made a profit nor a loss in August.
Profit/Loss = $0 (break-even).

**OBSERVATIONS / EXPLANATION**
-------------------------------
- Even without an explicit persona or CoT instruction, ChatGPT still
  produced a numbered breakdown here — but this is not guaranteed
  behavior for a "standard" prompt; it can vary run to run and is
  more likely to collapse steps or skip showing intermediate math on
  less structured problems.
- The main risk in this run type is that discount logic (per-order,
  not per-mug) can get silently misapplied since there is no explicit
  instruction to reason carefully before answering.
- There is no persona framing (e.g. "financial analyst"), so the tone
  stays generic rather than adopting a professional / audit-style
  format.
- Final answer reached: BREAK-EVEN ($0 profit/loss).
